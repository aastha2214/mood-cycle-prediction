# E7  Cross-sample validation (two independent groups of women from the same study)
Code: `src/ext_e7_cross_sample.py`, `src/ext_e7_shift.py`. Results: `results/ext_e7_*.csv`, `results/fig_e7_*.png`; report section 8.6.

**Terminology.** The study has two groups of women who never overlap: women with one session and women with up to three sessions. Training on one group and testing on the other is **cross-sample validation**. It is stronger than leave-one-woman-out (a different group of women, recruited under a different design), but it is **not cross-dataset validation**: same study (presumably the same laboratory, assays and recruitment period). A truly independent dataset would be needed for that claim.

**Protocol.** Direction 1: train on the single-session women, test on the longitudinal women. Direction 2: the reverse. In each direction the model never sees a test woman: an assertion checks that no woman appears on both sides. Everything that is learned from data, the mean, the standard deviation used to scale features, the hyper-parameters (chosen by cross-validation grouped by woman, inside the training group), is computed from the training group only. The baseline is the training-group mean, and R2 = 1 - SSE_model / SSE_baseline says how much better than that baseline the model predicts the other group (R2 measured against the test group's own mean is also given as a diagnostic, because it removes any difference in the average level between the groups).

**Uncertainty.** Women of the test group are resampled (with all their sessions) 2,000 times; paired comparisons use the same resampled women. For the hormone question, "hormones help" means: RMSE of phase + hormones (set C) minus RMSE of phase alone (set A) is negative. The p-value of a bootstrap difference d* is p = 2 min(P(d* <= 0), P(d* >= 0)) with +1 smoothing; the one-sided p for an improvement is P(d* >= 0). Multiple tests: **Holm** (controls the chance of any false positive) and **Benjamini-Hochberg FDR** (controls the expected share of false discoveries), over all 36 hormone comparisons and within each family of 18.

**Distribution shift.** *Standardised mean difference* = (mean in group 2 - mean in group 1) / pooled standard deviation: how many standard deviations apart the groups are; 0.2 is conventionally small, 0.5 medium. *Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic* = largest vertical gap between the two empirical distribution functions (0 = identical, 1 = completely separate); it is tested on one session per woman (her first) so that observations are independent. A *classifier two-sample test*: if a model can tell from the features which group a woman belongs to (area under the ROC curve above 0.5), the groups differ in the joint distribution of those features. *Relationship shift*: the same regression fitted separately in each group; a coefficient that differs between groups means the feature-outcome relationship itself is not the same. A shift can only explain a difference between the two directions if it is large enough in the right direction; with two groups this can be suggested, not proved.
